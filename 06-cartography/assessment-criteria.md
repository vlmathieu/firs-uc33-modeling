# Assessment criteria — cartography of models

*The cartography is 40 % of the course unit.*
*These criteria make the three expectations announced on 4 September explicit, and give them weights.*

---

## Weights

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---|
| Critical and comparative analysis | 40 % |
| Coverage and structure of the cartography | 25 % |
| Synthesis note: argument, clarity, three pages held | 20 % |
| Legibility and editorial choices of the A3 | 10 % |
| Traceability: sources cited, grid annexed | 5 % |

In a nutshell, your cartography should demonstrate your critical thinking on the following: what a model is; its objective; its mechanics; what it can and cannot do (scope and limits); and its usefulness.
Students who have been trained in FIRS **should never use a model blindly, but rather thoughtfully**.
They should also **remain critical of the results they obtain from a model** and not take them for granted.

---

## Critical and comparative analysis — 40 %

That criterion is the one that carries the most weight, and the one that separates a cartography from a catalogue.
It is assessed on the critical comparisons you make between models, not on what each model says about itself.

What earns marks here: assumptions put side by side and shown to conflict; a limit of one model stated by reference to what another does better; a model placed inside a published typology with the misfit named; a domain of validity that you have delineated rather than copied; a case where two models would answer the same question differently, and why; etc.

What loses them: paraphrasing the documentation, one model at a time, with no line connecting any two entries.

The `typology_position` and `what_does_not_fit` columns of the grid, and the comparative passages of the note, are where this criterion is read.

## Coverage and structure of the cartography — 25 %

Ten models is the floor, around a dozen the expectation, and they should span the programme rather than cluster in one course unit.
Coverage alone is not enough: the same reading grid has to be applied to every model, so that two entries can be compared without going back to the sources.

A grid adapted to your own set of models is better than the one handed out, provided the adaptation is deliberate, applied consistently and explained in the note.

## Synthesis note — 20 %

Three pages, PDF.
The note argues a point, it does not summarise the cartography.
The grid already holds the description, so a note that walks through the models in turn has wasted its three pages.

Assessed on whether there is a thesis, whether the evidence for it comes from your own entries, whether the structure of the cartography is justified, and whether the three pages hold. References do not count towards the limit.

## The A3 — 10 %

One page, PDF, orientation and layout free.
Assessed on whether it can be read: what is at the centre, what was deliberately dropped, whether the organising principle is visible without the note in hand.
A printed spreadsheet scores low here.
So does a design so inventive that no one can tell what claim it makes.
However, you are free to express your creativity when creating this cartography.

## Traceability — 5 %

Every model has a cited source, in a consistent format (*e.g.*, the APA format), with the primary reference distinguished from applications and documentation.
The filled grid is attached.
Ground rule of the opening session applies: cite every source, and be transparent about your use of AI tools.

## Procedure

Due 15 January, 23:59, Paris time, by email, with the three attachments named as set out in [`guidance-note.md`](guidance-note.md).

Flag a delay in advance.
Otherwise a penalty applies, typically 10 % per day, and beyond a limit the work may be refused.

The work is the pair's.
Tasks are split as you see fit, and each of you answers for the whole thing.
Tell us early if a pair stops working, rather than in January.

Your teachers are still available to help you and answer your questions.
